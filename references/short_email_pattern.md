# Short Outreach Sentence Pattern

Use this reference after building the runtime applicant profile. Treat every bracketed item below as an instruction, not text to leave in the final email.

## Core formula

Use:

`one target + one hook + up to two applicant proofs + one honest bridge + one ask`

The CV carries detailed courses, awards, project methods, internships, and publication lists.

## Chinese sentence architecture

1. **Salutation**
   `尊敬的[教授姓名]老师：`
   Verify the exact Chinese name.

2. **Purpose**
   Thank the professor briefly, then state the target cycle and opportunity directly.

3. **Applicant identity**
   Give name, current affiliation/status, and at most one strong numeric fact when the user wants it included.

4. **Primary proof**
   State the applicant's role, research topic or capability, and one verified outcome. Preserve the exact publication or project status.

5. **Optional secondary proof**
   Add one distinct capability only when it strengthens the target-side fit. Do not repeat the primary proof.

6. **Professor direction and hook**
   Name up to three current directions, then exactly one paper or active project. Explain one mechanism or contribution in one sentence.

7. **Fit bridge**
   Use one of these logics:

   - Direct: shared problem -> verified applicant capability -> target-side research value.
   - Adjacent: different setting -> transferable method -> concrete capability -> target-side research need.
   - Exploratory: limited overlap -> credible starting capability -> specific question the applicant wants to develop.

   Name the applicant work clearly after discussing the professor's work. Avoid ambiguous references such as “该项目” when two projects are in view. Do not end at “产生兴趣”; state what the applicant can already do and why it matters.

8. **Confirmed attachment**
   Mention only files that the user supplied or explicitly confirmed for the email.

9. **Low-friction ask**
   Ask for an opportunity to discuss fit and the target application or research arrangement. Mention a named lab only when its identity is confirmed.

10. **Closing and signature**
    Use one wishes line, then the applicant's preferred name, affiliation, optional email, optional phone/social contact, and date when requested.

## English architecture

Use the same evidence order in natural English rather than translating the Chinese sentences literally:

1. salutation and purpose;
2. current status and strongest proof;
3. one target hook and demonstrated understanding;
4. evidence-based fit bridge;
5. confirmed attachment and low-friction ask;
6. professional sign-off and minimal contact details.

## Writing rules

- Make each sentence perform one job.
- Prefer roles, methods, outcomes, and exact states over praise.
- Do not summarize multiple professor papers.
- Do not copy an abstract or over-explain the applicant's project.
- Do not claim an attachment merely because it is commonly expected.
- Do not put source links, citations, internal fit labels, or research notes in the email body.
- Recheck names, dates, metrics, status labels, and contact details immediately before finalizing.
- Remove every bracketed placeholder and any zero-width character.
